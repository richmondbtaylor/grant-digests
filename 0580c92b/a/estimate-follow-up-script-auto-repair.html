Estimate Follow-Up Script for Auto Repair Shops

Customers who receive estimates but don't return your calls represent lost revenue. A follow-up script bridges the gap between the estimate and the repair order. This article gives you practical steps to create and use follow-up scripts that work.

Why Follow-Up Scripts Matter

When a customer gets an estimate, they often need time to decide. They may be comparing prices, checking their budget, or waiting for approval from a spouse or fleet manager. Without a structured follow-up, you lose track of these opportunities. A script keeps your team consistent and ensures no estimate falls through the cracks.

Step 1: Set Your Follow-Up Timeline

Decide when you will contact customers after they receive an estimate. Most shops follow this pattern:

  • Same day or next morning: Initial estimate delivered
  • 24 to 48 hours later: First follow-up call
  • 5 to 7 days later: Second follow-up call
  • 10 to 14 days later: Final follow-up before marking estimate inactive

Write these dates on your calendar or set phone reminders. Consistency matters more than the exact timing.

Step 2: Create Your Core Follow-Up Script

Your script should be brief, friendly, and focused on the customer's decision. Here is a template you can use:

Opening: "Hi [Customer Name], this is [Your Name] from [Shop Name]. Do you have a quick minute? I wanted to follow up on the estimate we sent for your [Vehicle Year/Make/Model]."

Check-in: "Have you had a chance to review the estimate? Do you have any questions about the work or the price?"

Listen: Wait for the customer to respond. Do not interrupt. Common answers are:

  • "I haven't looked at it yet." (Reschedule follow-up for 2-3 days later)
  • "The price seems high." (Ask what they were quoted elsewhere, then explain your pricing)
  • "I need to talk to my spouse/manager." (Offer to call back at a better time)
  • "Yes, let's schedule the repair." (Book the appointment immediately)

Close: "Great, I'll [schedule your repair / send you more information / call back Thursday]. Thanks for choosing us."

Step 3: Build Scripts for Common Objections

Customers often raise the same concerns. Prepare responses in advance.

Objection: "Your price is too high."

Response: "I understand price matters. Our estimate includes [OEM parts / warranty / certified technician time]. What price were you quoted? Let me explain what makes our service worth the difference."

Objection: "I'm still thinking about it."

Response: "That's fair. What would help you decide? Is it the cost, or do you want to get another opinion? I'm happy to answer any questions."

Objection: "I'll call you back."

Response: "Perfect. When is a good time for me to check in with you if I don't hear from you by [specific day]?"

Objection: "I found someone cheaper."

Response: "I appreciate your honesty. Before you decide, can I ask what they quoted? Sometimes lower prices mean different parts or warranty coverage. Let me make sure you're comparing apples to apples."

Step 4: Assign Follow-Up Responsibility

Decide who makes the calls. Options include:

  • The service advisor who wrote the estimate
  • A dedicated follow-up person
  • The shop manager
  • A rotating team member

The service advisor is often best because they know the estimate details and the customer already trusts them. If you use a rotating system, make sure each person knows the script and has access to the estimate details.

Step 5: Track Your Follow-Ups

Create a simple tracking sheet. Use this format:

Customer Name Vehicle Estimate Date 1st Follow-Up Date 2nd Follow-Up Date Result John Smith 2019 Honda Civic Jan 10 Jan 12: No answer Jan 17: Scheduled Repair booked

Update this sheet after each call. Note what the customer said so the next person who calls knows the history.

Step 6: Use Multiple Contact Methods

Not every customer answers the phone. Try these approaches:

  • Call during business hours and after 5 p.m.
  • Send a text message: "Hi [Name], just checking in on your estimate. Any questions? Reply or call us."
  • Send an email with a link to schedule online
  • Leave a voicemail with a specific callback time

Combine methods for better results. A customer who misses your call might respond to a text.

Step 7: Know When to Stop

After three to four follow-up attempts over two weeks with no response, mark the estimate inactive. Store it in your system. If the customer calls later, you have the estimate ready. Do not keep calling after the customer has clearly declined.

Quick Checklist for Your First Week

  1. Write your core follow-up script and post it near the phone
  2. Create objection responses for your top three customer concerns
  3. Choose who will make follow-up calls
  4. Set calendar reminders for follow-up dates
  5. Create a simple tracking sheet or use your existing software
  6. Make your first follow-up call within 24 hours of the next estimate
  7. Record what happens and adjust your script based on results

Final Thoughts

Follow-up scripts work because they remove guesswork. Your team knows what to say, customers feel respected, and estimates convert to repairs more often. Start with the script and timeline above, then adjust based on your shop's style and what customers respond to.
