Expired Listing Cold Email Template That Works for Real Estate Agents

Expired listings represent one of the most underutilized lead sources in residential real estate. A property that failed to sell is a clear signal that the seller is frustrated, the listing agent may not have delivered results, and the homeowner is still motivated to move. Cold emails to expired listing owners can generate qualified leads and commissions, but only if you follow a proven structure.

This article walks you through building and sending expired listing cold emails that get responses. You will learn the framework, see real examples, and get a checklist you can use today.

Why Expired Listings Respond to Cold Email

When a listing expires, the seller has already invested time, money, and emotional energy into selling. They are not starting from scratch. They are frustrated. They are still motivated. They are also open to new approaches because the old one did not work. This mindset makes them far more likely to open and reply to a direct email from a new agent than a cold prospect with no history of trying to sell.

Cold email works for expired listings because it is non-intrusive, allows the seller to respond on their own time, and gives you space to explain what you would do differently.

The Core Structure of an Expired Listing Cold Email

Every effective expired listing email follows this five-part structure:

  1. Subject line that acknowledges the expiration
  2. Opening that shows you did your homework
  3. One clear reason why the listing expired
  4. Your specific solution
  5. A low-pressure call to action

Each part serves a purpose. The subject line gets the email opened. The opening builds credibility. The reason shows you understand their situation. The solution shows you have a plan. The call to action makes the next step obvious.

Subject Line Examples That Work

Your subject line must stand out in an inbox full of marketing emails. It should reference the property or the expiration directly. Generic subject lines like "Real Estate Help" or "Let's Talk" get deleted.

Strong subject lines for expired listings include:

  • 123 Oak Street Just Expired: New Strategy Inside
  • Your Home Didn't Sell: Here's Why (and How to Fix It)
  • 123 Oak Street: What the Last Agent Missed
  • Expired Listing at 123 Oak Street: Different Approach
  • Why 123 Oak Street Didn't Sell in 90 Days

Notice that each subject line is specific to the property and hints at a reason or solution. Avoid all caps, excessive punctuation, or words like "urgent" or "limited time."

The Opening: Show You Know the Property

The first sentence must prove you have looked at the listing and the property. Do not start with "I help sellers." Start with the property.

Weak opening: "Hi [Name], I am a real estate agent in your area and I help sellers get their homes sold."

Strong opening: "Hi [Name], I noticed your home at 123 Oak Street just expired after 90 days on the market. I pulled the listing and saw it has great bones, a large lot, and an updated kitchen."

The strong opening proves you looked at the actual listing. The seller knows you are not sending a mass email. This one detail increases your response rate.

State One Clear Reason Why It Expired

Do not list five problems. Pick one. The most common reasons expired listings fail are overpricing, poor marketing, poor photos, or bad timing. Choose the one that fits the data you can see.

Example: "Most homes in your neighborhood are selling between 450k and 480k. Your listing was priced at 520k, which put it outside the range most buyers are searching. That is likely why it did not get traction."

This shows you have done market research. It is specific. It is not accusatory. It explains the problem in a way the seller can understand and accept.

Present Your Solution

Your solution should be concrete and different from what the last agent did. Do not say "I will market it better." Say what you will actually do.

Example: "I would recommend repricing at 465k, which puts it in the active buying range for your neighborhood. I would also schedule a professional photo shoot and create a video walkthrough. Homes with video sell 30 percent faster in this market."

This tells the seller exactly what you will do and why. It is actionable. It is not a promise, but a plan.

The Call to Action

Do not ask for a meeting. Ask for a conversation. Make it easy to say yes.

Example: "Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call this week to talk through a new approach? I can work around your schedule."

This is low pressure. It is specific about time. It puts the seller in control. You are not demanding a listing. You are asking for a conversation.

Complete Email Example

Subject: 123 Oak Street Just Expired: New Strategy Inside

Body:

Hi Sarah,

I noticed your home at 123 Oak Street just expired after 90 days on the market. I pulled the listing and saw it has a beautiful updated kitchen, a large corner lot, and great natural light.

Looking at the market data for your neighborhood, I think I see why it did not sell. Homes in your area are moving between 450k and 480k. Your listing was at 520k, which put it outside the range most buyers are actively searching. That pricing gap is the main reason it did not get offers.

I would recommend repricing at 465k and doing a professional photo and video shoot. Homes with video in your market are selling 30 percent faster right now.

Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call this week to talk through a new approach? I can work around your schedule.

Best, [Your Name] [Your Phone]

Sending and Timing Your Emails

Send expired listing emails within one week of expiration. The frustration is fresh. The seller is most likely to be thinking about next steps.

Send on Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday between 9 a.m. and 11 a.m. These times have the highest open rates.

Space your emails out. Do not send 50 emails in one day. Send 5 to 10 per day over a week. This looks intentional, not like spam.

Follow-Up and Response Checklist

Create a simple tracking system. For each email sent, note the date, the property address, and the seller name. Follow up once after five days if you do not hear back. Keep the follow-up short.

Follow-up example: "Hi Sarah, just checking in on my email from last week about 123 Oak Street. Would this week work for a quick call? Thanks."

Do not send more than one follow-up. If they do not respond, move on.

Checklist for Your First Campaign

  • Find 10 expired listings in your area from the last 7 days
  • Pull the public listing data for each property
  • Research the market price range for each neighborhood
  • Write a custom subject line for each email
  • Write a custom opening that references the specific property
  • Identify one clear reason for the expiration
  • Write one specific solution
  • Write a low-pressure call to action
  • Send the email on Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday morning
  • Track the date and seller name in a spreadsheet
  • Follow up once after five days if no response
